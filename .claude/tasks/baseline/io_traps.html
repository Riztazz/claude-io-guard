First scope

  1. Command preflight before every Bash call: size, double backslash, shell writes to tracked files.Stops SHW-4, SHW-2 and SHW-1, the three largest token sinks in our data.
  2. Byte guard around Edit and Write, and the header line on every read.Stops every silent change of endings, BOM, encoding and size.
  3. Freshness gate, and a batch-edit primitive with exact-once anchors.Replaces the 73 edit() variants and most of the 417 writer scripts.
  4. Output capture with the real exit code.Stops OUT-1, OUT-2 and OUT-10.

The friend's first scope was the freshness gate, the byte guard, the shell-write policy and the exit-status wrapper. This one moves the command-size check to the front, because it is the largest token sink in our data and the cheapest check to build.
